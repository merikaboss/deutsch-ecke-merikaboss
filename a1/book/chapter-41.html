Like all German adjectives, color words are lowercase — never capitalized, even at the start of a color-focused sentence like gelb ist meine Lieblingsfarbe (yellow is my favorite color).

After "sein": no ending, same as Chapter 9

💬 Beispiele

  • Die Sonne ist gelb. (The sun is yellow.)
  • Der Himmel ist blau. (The sky is blue.)

Directly before a noun: now the ending finally matters

Chapter 9 promised this moment would come. When a color (or any adjective) sits directly in front of a noun, it takes an ending that matches the noun's gender and case — layered on top of everything you've already learned about ein/eine/ein (Chapter 11).

📐 Grammar rule: after ein/eine/ein, the adjective ending fills the gap the article left

GenderArticle ending (Ch. 11)Adjective endingExample
Masculineein (no ending)-erein blauer Stift (a blue pen)
Feminineeine (-e)-eeine blaue Tasche (a blue bag)
Neuterein (no ending)-esein blaues Heft (a blue notebook)
Plural— (no article)-eviele blaue Bücher (many blue books)

Notice the pattern: whenever the article itself gives no clear gender signal (masculine and neuter ein look identical), the adjective ending steps in to carry that information instead.

⚠️ Common mistake: using the same ending everywhere, like after "sein"

Since Chapter 9 taught you adjectives never change after sein, it's tempting to assume they never change at all. In front of a noun, they do: ein rotes Auto (a red car) but Das Auto ist rot (the car is red) — same word, different grammatical job.

Übungen (Exercises)

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Ergänzen Sie die Endung: ein grün___ Auto (das Auto). eine rot___ Tasche (die Tasche).
2. Übersetzen Sie beide Varianten: "The chair is black." und "This is a black chair."
3. Beantworten Sie auf Deutsch: Welche Farbe hat der Himmel? Welche Farbe hat Gras?
4. Fix the mistake: "Ich habe ein blau Heft."
5. Describe three objects around you right now, in German, using color adjectives directly before the noun (with the correct ending) rather than after "ist."
