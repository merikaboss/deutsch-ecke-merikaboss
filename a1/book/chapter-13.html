Die Uhrzeit

Telling time: official (24-hour) and everyday spoken time

Original topic sheet View "Die Uhrzeit" in the Picture Book →

German has two ways of telling time, and you need both: an official version used on TV, train stations, and official schedules, and an everyday spoken version used in casual conversation.

Official time: simple, and uses the 24-hour clock

This one is easy once you know your numbers (Chapter 2): just say the hour, then the minutes, then Uhr (o'clock/hour).

ClockGerman
14:00vierzehn Uhr
14:15vierzehn Uhr fünfzehn
14:30vierzehn Uhr dreißig
14:45vierzehn Uhr fünfundvierzig
09:05neun Uhr fünf

Since it uses 24-hour format, there's never any ambiguity about morning vs. evening — vierzehn Uhr can only mean 2pm, never 2am.

Everyday spoken time: 12-hour, and one tricky word

In casual speech, Germans use a 12-hour clock with some extra vocabulary for quarter-hours and half-hours.

ClockGermanLiteral meaning
3:00drei Uhr / Punkt dreithree o'clock / exactly three
3:15Viertel nach dreiquarter after three
3:45Viertel vor vierquarter before four
3:30halb vier"half four" — meaning half toward four

📐 Grammar rule: "halb" points forward, not backward

This is the single biggest trap for English speakers. English "half past three" looks back at 3:00. German halb vier looks forward to 4:00 — it means "halfway to four," i.e. 3:30. Saying halb drei when you mean 3:30 is a genuine, common mistake — it actually means 2:30.

⚠️ Common mistake: translating "half past three" literally

If it's 3:30 and you think "half past three," your instinct will be to say "halb drei." That's wrong — it means 2:30. For 3:30, you need halb vier — half-way to the next hour, not half of the current one.

Asking and answering "what time is it?"

💬 Examples

  • Wie spät ist es? (What time is it?)
  • Wie viel Uhr ist es? (What time is it? — alternative phrasing)
  • Es ist Viertel nach neun. (It's quarter past nine.)
  • Der Zug fährt um vierzehn Uhr dreißig. (The train leaves at 2:30pm.) — note "um" (at) before the time

Exercises

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Say 16:45 using official time.
2. Say 5:30 in everyday spoken German. Careful with "halb"!
3. What time does "halb acht" refer to?
4. Say "quarter to eight" in German.
5. Translate: "What time is it? It's 9:15."
6. Your train leaves at 18:20. Say this sentence in German using "um": "The train leaves at 18:20."
