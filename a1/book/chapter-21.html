Trennbare Verben

Separable verbs: prefixes that split apart and jump to the end

Original topic sheet View "Trennbare Verben" in the Picture Book →

Many German verbs are built from a small word (a "prefix") glued onto a base verb — an + kommen = ankommen (to arrive). In the infinitive, they look like one word. But the moment you conjugate them in a normal sentence, the prefix breaks off and jumps to the very end.

Separable prefixes (common)
ab-, an-, auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, vor-, zu-, zurück-, her-

📐 Grammar rule: conjugate the base verb normally, send the prefix to the end

The base verb takes the normal position-2 spot and gets conjugated exactly like any regular verb (Chapter 7). The prefix detaches and sits alone at the very end of the sentence — the same "verb bracket" pattern you saw with modal verbs in Chapter 16.

💬 Examples

InfinitiveIn a sentence
ankommen (to arrive)Der Zug kommt um 18 Uhr an. (The train arrives at 6pm.)
aufstehen (to get up)Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf. (I get up at seven.)
einkaufen (to shop)Wir kaufen heute ein. (We're going shopping today.)
anrufen (to call/phone)Ruf mich bitte an! (Please call me!)
mitkommen (to come along)Kommst du mit? (Are you coming along?)

⚠️ Common mistake: leaving the verb whole, like the infinitive

It's tempting to just say "Ich ankomme um 18 Uhr" — keeping the verb intact the way it looks in the dictionary. That's wrong; the prefix must separate: Ich komme um 18 Uhr an.

What happens with modal verbs

When a separable verb follows a modal verb (Chapter 16), it simply stays whole in its infinitive form at the end — the modal verb's infinitive slot already handles it, no separating needed: Ich muss morgen früh aufstehen. (I have to get up early tomorrow.)

Exercises

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Put into a sentence: ich / aufstehen / um sechs Uhr.
2. Translate: "The bus arrives at 8am." (use ankommen)
3. Fix the mistake: "Ich einkaufe heute."
4. Translate: "Are you (du) coming along?" (use mitkommen)
5. Translate, using a modal verb + separable verb: "I have to call my mother." (anrufen)
