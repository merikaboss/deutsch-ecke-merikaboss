Trennbare Verben

Separable verbs: when the prefix splits off, when it stays attached, and the prefixes you need most

Original topic sheet View "Trennbare Verben" in the Picture Book →

Many German verbs are made of two parts: a short Vorsilbe (prefix) and a normal verb. auf + stehen = aufstehen (to get up). In the dictionary they look like one word, but in most sentences the prefix breaks off and jumps to the end. These verbs are everywhere in daily life — getting up, calling someone, going shopping, picking someone up, getting on the bus — so you can't talk about your day without them. The logic is simple once you've seen it, and it's the same sentence bracket you already know from modal verbs.

1. What a separable verb is

Prefix + verb = a new meaning

The prefix usually changes the meaning of the base verb, sometimes a little, sometimes completely:

Base verbWith prefixMeaning
kommen (to come)ankommento arrive
mitkommento come along
zurückkommento come back
vorbeikommento drop by
machen (to do, make)aufmachento open
zumachento close
mitmachento take part, join in
hören (to hear)aufhörento stop
zuhörento listen

Separable prefixes are always stressed when spoken (Chapter 4): AUF-stehen, AN-kommen.

Forgetting the prefix changes the meaning

If you forget to put the prefix at the end, you've said a different verb. Ich höre um 5 Uhr. means nothing sensible ("I hear at 5"); Ich höre um 5 Uhr auf. means I stop at 5. When you listen to German, wait for the end of the sentence: the prefix there often tells you what the verb means.

2. Where the prefix goes: the four sentence types

The prefix splits off and goes to the end

TypeConjugated verbMiddleEnd
StatementIch stehejeden Tag um 6 Uhrauf.
Yes/no questionStehst duauch so frühauf?
W-questionWann stehst duauf?
CommandStehbitteauf!

The base verb is conjugated normally in its usual place (position 2, or position 1 in a yes/no question or command). The prefix waits at the very end, however long the sentence is: Ich rufe dich morgen nach der Arbeit gegen 18 Uhr an. (I'll call you tomorrow after work around 6 p.m.)

When the verb stays together — a correction to the sheet

The topic sheet says the prefix is "always" at the end of the sentence. That's true for the four types above, but in these three situations the verb stays in one piece at the end:

SituationExampleEnglish
After a modal verb (Ch. 16)Ich muss morgen früh aufstehen.I have to get up early tomorrow.
In a subordinate clause (weil, dass, wenn)Ich bin müde, weil ich früh aufstehe.I'm tired because I get up early.
In the Perfekt (Ch. 28–29)Ich bin um 6 Uhr aufgestanden.I got up at 6.

In the Perfekt the -ge- goes between prefix and verb: aufgestanden, angerufen, eingekauft.

The four typical mistakes

  • Not separating: "Ich aufstehe um 7." → Ich stehe um 7 auf.
  • Prefix too early: "Ich stehe auf um 7." → the prefix goes to the very end: Ich stehe um 7 auf.
  • Separating after a modal: "Ich muss stehe auf." → Ich muss aufstehen.
  • Forgetting the prefix: "Der Zug kommt um 8." is understandable, but the precise "arrives" is Der Zug kommt um 8 an.

Vowel changes stay in separable verbs

If the base verb changes its vowel (Chapter 8), the separable verb does too:

  • fernsehen → Er sieht jeden Abend fern. (He watches TV every evening.)
  • einladen → Sie lädt uns zum Essen ein. (She invites us for dinner.)
  • anfangen → Der Film fängt um acht an. (The film starts at eight.)
  • mitnehmen → Nimmst du den Schirm mit? (Are you taking the umbrella?)
  • abfahren → Der Zug fährt gleich ab. (The train is about to depart.)

A few prefixes, such as um- and über-, can be either, depending on the verb: Ich ziehe um (I'm moving house) is separable, but umarmen (to hug) is not. At A1 the verbs in the table above are the ones that matter.

5. nicht and separable verbs

nicht goes before the prefix at the end, or before whatever specific part it negates: Er ruft mich nicht an. (He doesn't call me.) · Ich stehe heute nicht früh auf. (I'm not getting up early today.) · Kommst du nicht mit? (Aren't you coming along?)

6. In context

Mein Samstag (my Saturday)

Am Samstag stehe ich spät auf, meistens um neun. Zuerst räume ich die Wohnung auf. Dann kaufe ich im Supermarkt ein. Am Nachmittag rufe ich meine Mutter in Tansania an. Am Abend lädt mich meine Freundin oft zum Essen ein, und ich bringe einen Nachtisch mit. Manchmal gehen wir danach aus. Wenn ich müde bin, sehe ich lieber fern. Um Mitternacht mache ich den Fernseher aus und schlafe sofort ein.

(On Saturday I get up late, usually at nine. First I tidy up the flat. Then I do the shopping at the supermarket. In the afternoon I phone my mother in Tanzania. In the evening my friend often invites me for dinner, and I bring a dessert. Sometimes we go out afterwards. If I'm tired, I'd rather watch TV. At midnight I switch off the TV and fall asleep immediately.)
