Unbestimmte Artikel

Indefinite articles: ein, eine, ein — "a/an"

Original topic sheet View "Unbestimmter Artikel" in the Picture Book →

Where der/die/das point to a specific, already-known thing ("the table"), ein/eine/ein introduces something new or unspecified ("a table" — any table, one you haven't mentioned yet).

GenderDefinite (Ch. 10)IndefiniteExample
Masculinedereinein Tisch (a table)
Femininedieeineeine Tasche (a bag)
Neuterdaseinein Buch (a book)

Grammar rule: there is no plural indefinite article

English has no trouble with this either — you can't say "a books." German plurals simply drop the article entirely when the meaning is "some/any," rather than "the": Bücher (books / some books), not "eine Bücher."

Examples

  • Was ist das? Das ist ein Baum. (What is that? That is a tree.)
  • Ich habe eine Schwester. (I have a sister.)
  • Das sind Bücher. (Those are books.) — plural, no article at all

Common mistake: using "ein" with plural nouns

Because ein means "a/an" and English speakers are used to needing some determiner before a noun, there's a temptation to invent a plural form. There isn't one — just drop the article: Ich kaufe Äpfel (I'm buying apples), no article needed at all.

A preview: negating with "kein"

The negative counterpart of ein/eine/ein is kein/keine/kein (not a / no) — its own full topic in Chapter 12, right after this one.
