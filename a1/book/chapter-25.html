Präteritum — war und hatte

The simple past of sein and haben

Original topic sheet View "Past Tense – Waren und Hatten" in the Picture Book →

German has two past tenses used in everyday speech, and this is the first: the Präteritum (simple past). In practice, it's reserved almost entirely for sein and haben — every other verb's everyday past uses the Perfekt tense instead, which starts in Chapter 28. So this chapter's two verbs are worth memorizing solidly on their own.

📐 Grammar rule: when to use war vs. hatte

Use war for places, movement, states of being, and time/weather. Use hatte for possessions, general past actions, age, and feelings/ailments.

💬 Beispiele mit "war"

  • Ich war gestern zu Hause. (I was at home yesterday.)
  • Er war müde. (He was tired.)
  • Das Wetter war schön. (The weather was nice.)
  • Wann warst du in Deutschland? (When were you in Germany?)

💬 Beispiele mit "hatte"

  • Ich hatte einen Hund. (I had a dog.)
  • Wir hatten viel Arbeit. (We had a lot of work.)
  • Er hatte Kopfschmerzen. (He had a headache.)
  • Ich hatte 18 Jahre. (I was 18 years old — literally "I had 18 years.")

⚠️ Common mistake: reaching for the Perfekt with sein/haben too

Once you learn the Perfekt tense (Chapter 28), it's tempting to say "ich bin gewesen" for every past "I was." That's grammatically valid but sounds stiff and unnatural in everyday speech — native speakers almost always say simply ich war. Keep the Präteritum as your default for sein and haben specifically.

Übungen (Exercises)

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Ergänzen Sie mit "war" oder "waren": Ich ___ gestern in der Schule. Wir ___ einen schönen Urlaub.
2. Ergänzen Sie mit "hatte" oder "hatten": Du ___ ein neues Handy. Ich ___ gestern Kopfschmerzen.
3. Übersetzen Sie: "We were in the park. It was cold."
4. Übersetzen Sie: "I had a dog when I was young." (use hatte and war in the same sentence)
5. Beantworten Sie auf Deutsch: Wo warst du letzte Woche? Was hattest du zum Frühstück (breakfast) gestern?
6. Write four sentences about your own week last week, alternating between war/waren and hatte/hatten at least once each.
