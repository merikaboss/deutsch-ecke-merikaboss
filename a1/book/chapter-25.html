Präteritum – war und hatte

The simple past of sein and haben, es gab, time words for the past, and talking about how things used to be

Original topic sheet View "Past Tense – Waren und Hatten" in the Picture Book →

This is your first step into the past. German has two main past tenses. The Perfekt (Chapters 28–29) is used for most verbs in everyday speech. The Präteritum (simple past) is used in books and news, but in speech Germans use it for a small group of very common verbs, above all sein (to be) and haben (to have). So war (was) and hatte (had) are the natural way to say "I was" and "I had", and you'll use them constantly: Where were you? I was ill. I had no time. It was great.

1. The forms

sein → war and haben → hatte

Two patterns make these easy. First, just like the modal verbs, ich and er/sie/es are identical with no ending: ich war, er war; ich hatte, sie hatte. Second, the endings are the ones you know: -st, -en, -t, -en.

Spelling: wart, not "ward"

ihr wart (you all were) ends in t. And hatte always has double t: hatte, hattest, hatten.

2. When to use war and when to use hatte

The choice is simple: use war wherever you'd use sein in the present, and hatte wherever you'd use haben. Just put the present sentence into the past:

Important corrections to the topic sheet and the old chapter

  • Age uses sein, never haben. The sheet gives „Ich hatte 18 Jahre alt", and the earlier version of this chapter said „Ich hatte 18 Jahre". Both are wrong. The correct sentence is Ich war 18 Jahre alt. Age works in the past exactly as in the present (Chapter 7).
  • hatte does not mean "I ate, I drank, I played". It means only "had". "I ate" is the Perfekt ich habe gegessen (Chapter 28). The sheet's list "things in general (I ate, I drank, I played)" mixes up hatte with the Perfekt helper verb haben.
  • war does not mean "I went". "I went" is ich bin gegangen. The sheet's "movement (I went)" again confuses war with the Perfekt helper sein.
  • You have a holiday with haben: the sheet's exercise „Wir ___ einen schönen Urlaub" needs hatten: Wir hatten einen schönen Urlaub. (You were on holiday: Wir waren im Urlaub.)
  • The sheet's exercise 7, „Ihr ___ einen guten Film gesehen", is the Perfekt, so the answer is habt, not a past form. It doesn't belong in this exercise.

3. es gab: "there was" / "there were"

The past of es gibt

es gibt (there is / there are) from Chapter 6 becomes es gab in the past. Like the present, it never changes for plural and takes the accusative:

  • Früher gab es hier einen Supermarkt. (There used to be a supermarket here.)
  • Auf der Party gab es viel Essen. (There was a lot of food at the party.)
  • Gestern gab es einen Unfall. (There was an accident yesterday.)
  • Es gab keine Probleme. (There were no problems.)

4. Other verbs in the simple past (a reminder)

Besides war, hatte and gab, the verbs Germans normally put in the simple past when speaking are the modal verbs, from Chapter 16: konnte, musste, durfte, sollte, wollte.

All together

Letzte Woche war ich krank. Ich hatte Fieber und konnte nicht arbeiten. Ich musste zum Arzt. Zum Glück gab es in der Nähe eine Praxis. (Last week I was ill. I had a fever and couldn't work. I had to go to the doctor. Luckily there was a practice nearby.)

letzte / letzten / letztes are accusative adjective endings (Chapter 9): letzte Woche (feminine), letzten Monat (masculine), letztes Jahr (neuter). vor + dative means "ago" (Chapter 17).

6. Sentences with war and hatte

Same word order as the present

  • Statement, verb in position 2: Gestern war ich im Kino. (Yesterday I was at the cinema.)
  • Yes/no question: Warst du schon in Berlin? (Have you ever been to Berlin?)
  • W-question: Wo wart ihr am Wochenende? (Where were you at the weekend?)
  • Negation: Ich war nicht zu Hause. · Ich hatte keine Zeit.
  • After weil, verb last: Ich konnte nicht kommen, weil ich krank war.

"When I was ...": als

To talk about a time in your life, use als (when). It starts a subordinate clause, so the verb goes to the end:

  • Als ich Kind war, hatte ich einen Hund. (When I was a child, I had a dog.)
  • Als ich 20 war, war ich Student in Kampala. (When I was 20, I was a student in Kampala.)

Notice "verb, comma, verb" when the als clause comes first (Chapter 5).

Don't say "ich bin gewesen" for every "I was"

Once you learn the Perfekt, ich bin gewesen and ich habe gehabt are grammatically possible. But in most regions they sound heavy. Native speakers nearly always say simply ich war and ich hatte. Keep these two verbs in the simple past.

7. Früher und heute (then and now)

Mein Leben früher und heute

Früher war mein Leben ganz anders. Als ich Kind war, wohnten wir in einem kleinen Dorf in Kenia. Wir hatten kein Auto, aber wir hatten viele Tiere: zwei Kühe, Hühner und einen Hund. Im Dorf gab es nur eine Schule und keinen Supermarkt. Meine Großmutter war sehr wichtig für mich. Sie hatte immer Zeit und kannte viele Geschichten.

Heute bin ich 34 Jahre alt und wohne in Stuttgart. Ich habe eine Wohnung und ein Auto, aber keine Tiere. Das Leben hier ist schnell. Ich habe oft wenig Zeit. Letztes Jahr war ich wieder im Dorf. Es war wunderschön, aber es war auch komisch: Meine Großmutter war nicht mehr da.

(In the past my life was very different. When I was a child we lived in a small village in Kenya. We had no car, but we had lots of animals: two cows, chickens and a dog. In the village there was only one school and no supermarket. My grandmother was very important to me. She always had time and knew many stories. Today I'm 34 and live in Stuttgart. I have a flat and a car, but no animals. Life here is fast. I often have little time. Last year I was back in the village. It was wonderful, but also strange: my grandmother wasn't there any more.)

You'll see wohnten and kannte in written texts like this. They're simple past forms of other verbs; in speech you'd usually say wir haben gewohnt (Chapter 28).
