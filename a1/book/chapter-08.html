Unregelmäßige Verben

Irregular verbs: the vowel changes du and he/she/it (in fact just they) get

Original topic sheet View "Verben mit Stammveränderung" in the Picture Book →

A group of very common German verbs change their stem vowel — but only in the du and er/sie/es forms. Every other form (ich, wir, ihr, sie/Sie) still uses the regular endings from Chapter 7. There's no way to predict which verbs do this from the infinitive alone — you learn them individually, but they fall into three clear patterns.

Pattern 1: a → ä

Infinitiveichduer/sie/es
fahren (to drive/go)fahrefährstfährt
schlafen (to sleep)schlafeschläfstschläft
tragen (to carry/wear)trageträgstträgt

Pattern 2: e → i

Infinitiveichduer/sie/es
sprechen (to speak)sprechesprichstspricht
essen (to eat)essedu isst (no extra "essst")isst
nehmen (to take — irregular within irregular)nehmenimmstnimmt
helfen (to help)helfehilfsthilft

Pattern 3: e → ie

Infinitiveichduer/sie/es
lesen (to read)leseliestliest
sehen (to see)sehesiehstsieht

Common mistake: applying the vowel change to "ich"

The vowel change only happens in du and er/sie/es. Saying "ich spriche" instead of the correct ich spreche is a very common slip — the ich-form always stays regular.

Examples in full sentences

  • Fährst du nach Berlin? (Are you driving to Berlin?)
  • Er spricht sehr gut Englisch. (He speaks English very well.)
  • Sie liest ein Buch. (She is reading a book.)
  • Was isst du? (What are you eating?)

Exercises

Try it yourself

1. Conjugate fahren for du and er.
2. Conjugate sprechen for ich and sie (she).
3. Conjugate lesen for du.
4. Spot and fix the mistake: "Ich schläfe viel."
5. Translate: "He takes the book. She eats an apple. Do you (du) see the car?"
