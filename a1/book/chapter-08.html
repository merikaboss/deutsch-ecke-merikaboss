Unregelmäßige Verben

Verbs that change their vowel: the real patterns, the full lists, and the special cases

Original topic sheet View "Verben mit Stammveränderung" in the Picture Book →

In Chapter 7 you learned that most verbs keep the same stem and just add endings. A group of very common verbs does something extra: in two of the six forms, the vowel inside the stem changes. Ich fahre (I drive), but du fährst (you drive). This is called a Stammvokalwechsel (stem-vowel change). Some of the most useful verbs in German work like this — to eat, to see, to read, to speak, to take, to give, to drive, to sleep — so this chapter is worth learning thoroughly.

1. The one rule that makes it simple

Only du and er / sie / es change

The vowel change happens only in the du form and the er / sie / es form. The other four forms — ich, wir, ihr, sie / Sie — are completely regular. The endings are always the normal ones from Chapter 7.

Important corrections to the topic sheet

The sheet for this topic contains several errors. Please follow this chapter instead:

  • The ihr form never changes in the present tense. The sheet says "sometimes in ihr" — it's always ihr fahrt, ihr esst, ihr seht.
  • kommen, freuen, kaufen, wohnen, besuchen, brauchen, fragen do not change. It's du kommst, sie freut sich, er kauft, du wohnst, er fragt. They are regular verbs.
  • The patterns "o → ö" and "eu → äu" don't exist as normal A1 patterns; only one rare verb (stoßen, to push → er stößt) changes o → ö. And au → äu applies to laufen (to run), not to kaufen.
  • müssen is a modal verb with its own pattern, where even ich changes (ich muss). It's in Chapter 16.
  • gehen and trinken are regular in the present (du gehst, er trinkt). They only change in the past tenses.

How do you know if an e verb takes i or ie? You can't reliably guess, but a good hint: if the vowel in the infinitive is long (lesen, sehen), it usually becomes long ie. If it is short (sprechen, helfen), it becomes short i. geben and nehmen are the exceptions.

3. The spelling details inside the patterns

Several verbs combine the vowel change with the spelling adjustments from Chapter 7. Look closely at these, because they are where most written mistakes happen:

Watch the endings

  • s-sound stems: essen → du isst (not "issst"), lesen → du liest, lassen → du lässt. The du and er forms look identical.
  • t-stems lose the extra e: regular arbeiten gives er arbeitet, but halten gives er hält (not "hältet"), du hältst.
  • nehmen changes twice: the vowel becomes i and the h becomes a double m: du nimmst, er nimmt.

wissen and mögen change even in the ich form, and their ich and er forms have no ending at all: ich weiß, er weiß. This is the same pattern as the modal verbs in Chapter 16. Examples: Ich weiß es nicht. (I don't know.) · Er wird Arzt. (He's becoming a doctor.) · Magst du Pizza? (Do you like pizza?)

5. How to learn which verbs change

There is no rule that tells you from the infinitive alone. The practical solution used by dictionaries and textbooks: learn the er form together with the infinitive. Many dictionaries write it in brackets: fahren (fährt), lesen (liest). If the bracket shows a different vowel, it's a changing verb. If no bracket is given, it's regular.

Longer verbs keep the change

A verb built on a changing verb changes too, even with a prefix in front:

  • abfahren (to depart) → Der Zug fährt um 9 Uhr ab. (The train departs at 9.)
  • einladen (to invite) → Sie lädt mich ein. (She invites me.)
  • anfangen (to begin) → Der Kurs fängt an. (The course begins.)
  • vergessen (to forget) → Er vergisst alles. (He forgets everything.)

The two most common mistakes

  • Changing the ich form: "ich spriche", "ich fähre". The ich form stays regular: ich spreche, ich fahre.
  • Forgetting the change with er: "er esst", "sie sehet". Correct: er isst, sie sieht.

Ein normaler Tag (a normal day)

Jonas schläft lange. Dann liest er die Zeitung und isst ein Brötchen. Um neun Uhr fährt er mit dem Fahrrad zur Arbeit. Er spricht dort viel Englisch, denn er hilft internationalen Kunden. Am Abend trifft er Freunde, oder er sieht fern. Und du? Wann schläfst du, und was liest du gern?

(Jonas sleeps late. Then he reads the newspaper and eats a bread roll. At nine he cycles to work. He speaks a lot of English there because he helps international customers. In the evening he meets friends, or he watches TV. And you? When do you sleep, and what do you like reading?)
