Modalverben (möchten)

Modal verbs: can, must, want, should, may, would like

Original topic sheet View "Modalverben" in the Picture Book →

Modal verbs express ability, necessity, desire, permission, or advice — and they change German sentence structure in an important, predictable way.

Common mistake: expecting "ich" and "er/sie/es" to differ

Notice ich kann and er kann are identical — no -t ending, unlike regular verbs. This is true for every modal verb except möchten. It looks like a mistake to write "ich kann" and "er kann" the same way, but it's correct.

Grammar rule: modal verb + infinitive at the very end

The modal verb takes the normal position-2 spot (Chapter 5), while the main verb goes to the very end of the sentence in its plain infinitive form (unconjugated, -en ending). This "verb bracket" — modal near the front, infinitive at the end, everything else sandwiched in the middle — is a core German sentence pattern you'll see again with separable verbs (Chapter 21) and the perfect tense (Chapters 28–29).

Examples

  • Ich kann gut Deutsch sprechen. (I can speak German well.)
  • Wir müssen jetzt gehen. (We have to go now.)
  • Er will ein neues Auto kaufen. (He wants to buy a new car.)
  • Darf ich hier rauchen? (Am I allowed to smoke here?)
  • Ich möchte einen Kaffee bestellen. (I would like to order a coffee.)

möchten vs. mögen: "would like" vs. "like"

möchten expresses a specific, current wish ("I'd like a coffee right now"), while mögen (to like, in general) expresses a general preference and often doesn't need an infinitive at all: Ich mag Kaffee (I like coffee, in general) vs. Ich möchte einen Kaffee (I'd like a coffee, right now).

Exercises

Try it yourself

1. Conjugate müssen for du and wir.
2. Build a sentence: ich / können / gut / kochen (I can cook well).
3. Translate: "Can you (du) help me?"
4. Translate: "We want to learn German."
5. Fix the mistake: "Ich möchte trinke ein Wasser." (Hint: where should the second verb go, and in what form?)
6. Translate, using möchten: "I would like to order a pizza."
