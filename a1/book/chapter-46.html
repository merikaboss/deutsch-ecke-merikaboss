Grammar rule: "gern" = doing something gladly/enjoying it

To say you like doing something (as opposed to liking a thing, Chapter 34), German adds gern after the verb — it's an adverb, so it never changes form, and it sits in the normal adverb position (usually right after the conjugated verb).

GermanEnglish
Ich esse gern Obst.I like eating fruit.
Ich trinke gern Wasser.I like drinking water.
Was isst du gern?What do you like to eat?

Beispiel-Gespräch

A: Was isst du zum Frühstück?
A: What do you eat for breakfast?

B: Ich esse Brot, Käse und ein Ei.
B: I eat bread, cheese, and an egg.

A: Was trinkst du gern?
A: What do you like to drink?

B: Ich trinke gern Kaffee.
B: I like drinking coffee.

Common mistake: confusing "gern" with "gerne haben" (to like a thing)

gern attaches to a verb (an activity): Ich esse gern Pizza (I like eating pizza). To say you like a thing without a verb, go back to Chapter 34's mögen: Ich mag Pizza. Both are correct, but they're not interchangeable structures — "Ich gern Pizza" with no verb is not a valid sentence.

Übungen (Exercises)

Try it yourself

1. Übersetzen Sie mit "gern": "I like drinking tea."
2. Beantworten Sie auf Deutsch: Was isst du gern zum Frühstück? Was trinkst du nicht gern?
3. Fix the mistake: "Ich gern esse Obst." (Word order — where does "gern" actually go?)
4. Erklären Sie den Unterschied zwischen "Ich esse gern Pizza" und "Ich mag Pizza." (Explain the structural difference between the two.)
5. Write a short dialogue (4–5 lines) discussing what you and a friend like to eat and drink for lunch, using "gern" at least twice.
