Personalpronomen im Akkusativ und Dativ

Object pronouns: me, you, him, her... in the accusative and dative

Original topic sheet View "Personalpronomen – Nominativ, Akkusativ, Dativ" in the Picture Book →

Chapter 6 covered subject pronouns (nominative). Now that you've met the accusative and dative cases in Chapter 14, here's how the pronouns themselves change shape in each — this is what lets you say "he sees me" or "she helps him" without repeating the noun.

NominativeAccusativeDativeEnglish
ichmichmirI / me / to me
dudichdiryou / you / to you
erihnihmhe / him / to him
siesieihrshe / her / to her
esesihmit / it / to it
wirunsunswe / us / to us
ihreucheuchyou all / you all / to you all
sie / Siesie / Sieihnen / Ihnenthey/you / them/you / to them/you

💬 Examples

  • Kennst du ihn? (Do you know him?) — accusative, "ihn" is what's known
  • Ich helfe dir gern. (I'm happy to help you.) — dative, because "helfen" always takes dative (a small list of verbs do this — you just learn them)
  • Er schreibt mir jeden Tag. (He writes to me every day.) — dative, "to me"
  • Sie liebt ihn. (She loves him.) — accusative

📐 Grammar rule: some verbs always take the dative, even where English wouldn't expect it

helfen (to help), danken (to thank), and gefallen (to be pleasing to / to like) all take a dative object in German, even though English treats "help him" and "thank her" as direct objects. There's no shortcut here — these are memorized case-by-verb, one of the genuinely arbitrary parts of German grammar.

⚠️ Common mistake: "Ich helfe ihn" instead of "Ich helfe ihm"

Because "help him" feels like a normal direct object in English, it's very common to say "ich helfe ihn" (accusative). The correct form is ich helfe ihm (dative) — because helfen is one of the dative verbs.

gefallen: a useful, oddly-structured verb

gefallen literally means "to be pleasing to" and flips the structure English uses for "like": Der Film gefällt mir literally means "the movie is pleasing to me," used to say "I like the movie." The thing liked is the subject; the person doing the liking is in the dative.

Exercises

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Translate: "Do you (du) know her?"
2. Fill in the correct pronoun: Ich danke ___ (you, formal).
3. Translate: "He helps me every day." (Careful with the case!)
4. Translate using "gefallen": "I like the book" (literally: "the book is pleasing to me").
5. Fix the mistake: "Kannst du helfen mich?"
