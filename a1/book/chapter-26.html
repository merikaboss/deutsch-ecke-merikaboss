Untrennbare Verben

The eight prefixes that never separate, how they change meaning, and how to tell them from separable verbs

Original topic sheet View "Untrennbare Verben" in the Picture Book →

In Chapter 21 you met prefixes that break off and jump to the end: Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf. This chapter is about the opposite group: a small, fixed set of prefixes that never separate. verstehen (to understand) always stays verstehe, verstehst, versteht. Many of the most useful verbs you know belong here — to visit, to pay, to order, to understand, to forget, to explain, to get — so recognising this group saves you from a whole class of mistakes, and it becomes essential for the past tense in Chapter 28.

Important corrections to the topic sheet

The sheet for this topic contains two serious errors. Please follow this chapter instead:

  • einkaufen is separable, not inseparable. The sheet's main example, „ein + kaufen = einkaufen", is actually a separable verb: Ich kaufe heute ein. ein- is a separable prefix (Chapter 21). The sheet's box "einkaufen ✓ / ein kaufen ✗" is also misleading, because in a sentence einkaufen does split.
  • Most verbs on the sheet's list have no prefix at all. arbeiten, wohnen, lernen, kaufen, mieten, wissen, glauben, sehen, hören, bleiben, schlafen, helfen, brauchen and passieren are simple verbs. They can't be split because there's nothing to split, but that doesn't make them "inseparable verbs" in the grammatical sense. Only verstehen and gefallen on that list are true inseparable-prefix verbs.

1. The eight inseparable prefixes

be- · emp- · ent- · er- · ge- · miss- · ver- · zer-

A verb starting with one of these eight prefixes never separates, in any tense or sentence type. You conjugate it exactly like a simple verb. Three features help you recognise them:

  1. They are not words on their own. There's no German word "be" or "ver". Separable prefixes, by contrast, are real words: an, auf, mit, aus.
  2. They are never stressed. The stress goes on the stem: be-SU-chen, ver-STE-hen, er-KLÄ-ren. Separable prefixes are stressed: AN-kommen.
  3. They take no ge- in the Perfekt: besucht, verstanden, erklärt (section 5).

A memory help: "Be Emperor, Enter Germany, Miss Very Zero" (be-, emp-, ent-, er-, ge-, miss-, ver-, zer-).

False friend: bekommen ≠ "become"

bekommen means to get / to receive. Ich bekomme ein Kind means "I'm having a baby", not "I become a child". "To become" is werden: Ich werde Ärztin. (I'm becoming a doctor.) In a café, Ich bekomme einen Kaffee is a normal way to order: "I'll get a coffee".

3. Same stem, different prefix, different meaning

Seeing one stem with several prefixes, separable and inseparable, is the best way to feel how prefixes work:

Verb families

stehen (stand)verstehen (understand)aufstehen (get up)

A prefix can change the case: antworten vs beantworten

antworten takes the dative for the person, and auf + accusative for the question: Ich antworte dir. · Ich antworte auf die Frage. With be-, the question becomes a direct accusative object: Ich beantworte die Frage. This is what "be- makes a verb take a direct object" means in practice.

4. Inseparable verbs in every sentence type

They never split

Sentence typeExampleEnglish
StatementIch verstehe die Frage nicht.I don't understand the question.
QuestionBezahlst du mit Karte?Are you paying by card?
CommandVergiss das nicht!Don't forget that!
With a modalIch muss die Rechnung bezahlen.I have to pay the bill.
Subordinate clause..., weil ich die Frage nicht verstehe.because I don't understand the question.
PerfektIch habe die Frage nicht verstanden.I didn't understand the question.

Vowel changes stay

If the base verb changes its vowel (Chapter 8), the inseparable verb does too:

  • empfehlen → du empfiehlst, er empfiehlt
  • vergessen → du vergisst, sie vergisst · imperative Vergiss!
  • gefallen → Das gefällt mir.
  • verlassen (to leave a place) → er verlässt das Haus

5. Why this matters for the past tense

No ge- in the Perfekt

In Chapter 28 you'll build past participles with ge-: machen → gemacht. Verbs with an inseparable prefix never add ge-, because the prefix already fills that position:

TypeInfinitiveParticiple
simplesuchengesucht
separableaussuchenausgesucht (ge in the middle)
inseparablebesuchenbesucht (no ge)
inseparableverstehenverstanden
inseparablebekommen · vergessenbekommen · vergessen (same as the infinitive!)

The same applies to verbs ending in -ieren (studiert), covered in Chapter 29.

6. Separable or inseparable?

A four-step check

  1. Does the verb start with be-, emp-, ent-, er-, ge-, miss-, ver-, zer-? → inseparable.
  2. Does it start with a real small word like an, auf, aus, ein, mit, vor, zu, zurück, weg, los? → separable.
  3. Is there no prefix at all (arbeiten, lernen, schlafen)? → a simple verb; nothing to separate.
  4. Does it start with durch-, über-, um-, unter-, wieder-? → it can be either; check a dictionary. At A1, umsteigen and umziehen are separable, while übersetzen (to translate) and wiederholen (to repeat) are inseparable.

Both kinds in one verb

A few verbs have a separable prefix and an inseparable one, such as vorbereiten (to prepare) = vor- + be- + reiten. The outer prefix separates, the inner one stays: Ich bereite das Essen vor. And because of be-, the participle has no ge-: vorbereitet.

7. Common mistakes

Watch for these

  • "Ich stehe das nicht ver." → Ich verstehe das nicht.
  • "Ich bekomme müde." (for "I'm getting tired") → Ich werde müde.
  • "Ich antworte die Frage." → Ich beantworte die Frage or Ich antworte auf die Frage.
  • "Ich habe meine Oma gebesucht." → Ich habe meine Oma besucht.

8. In context

Im Fundbüro (at the lost property office)

GermanEnglish
Frau Nkosi: Guten Tag. Ich verliere nie etwas, aber heute habe ich meine Tasche vergessen.Hello. I never lose anything, but today I forgot my bag.
Mitarbeiter: Wo haben Sie sie vergessen?Where did you leave it?
Frau Nkosi: In der Straßenbahn. Ich musste mich beeilen, ich wollte den Zug nicht verpassen.On the tram. I had to hurry; I didn't want to miss the train.
Mitarbeiter: Beschreiben Sie die Tasche, bitte.Please describe the bag.
