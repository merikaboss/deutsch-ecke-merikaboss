Adjektive und Gegenteile

Describing people and things, opposites, comparing, and adjective endings in all four cases

Original topic sheet View "Adjektive und Gegenteile" in the Picture Book →

Adjektive sagen, wie etwas ist. (Adjectives tell us what something is like.) With adjectives you can describe a flat you want to rent, say a jacket is too expensive, say you are happy or tired, and compare two things. This chapter works in three steps: first the easy pattern where adjectives never change, then comparing (bigger, the biggest), and finally the endings adjectives take when they stand in front of a noun, in all four cases.

1. The easy pattern: after sein, no endings

Adjectives after the verb don't change

When an adjective comes after sein (to be), werden (to become) or bleiben (to stay), it stays in its dictionary form. Gender, number and case make no difference:

  • Der Hund ist groß. (The dog is big.)
  • Die Katze ist klein. (The cat is small.)
  • Das Haus ist schön. (The house is beautiful.)
  • Die Bücher sind alt. (The books are old.)
  • Es wird kalt. (It's getting cold.)

Adjective and adverb are the same word

English turns quick into quickly. German doesn't. The same plain form describes a noun and describes how an action is done:

  • Das Auto ist schnell. (The car is fast.)
  • Er fährt schnell. (He drives fast / quickly.)
  • Sie singt schön. (She sings beautifully.)
  • Du sprichst gut Deutsch. (You speak German well.)

Notice how many opposites are made with un-, like English "un-". You can often build one yourself: wichtig (important) → unwichtig (unimportant).

False friends

  • brav means well-behaved (a child), not "brave". Brave is mutig.
  • sensibel means sensitive, not "sensible". Sensible is vernünftig.
  • fast is not an adjective at all: it means almost. Fast is schnell.
  • alt works for people and things (old), but its opposite depends: a person is jung, a thing is neu.

Making adjectives stronger or weaker

From gar nicht to sehr

GermanEnglish
Das ist gar nicht teuer.That's not expensive at all.
Das ist nicht so teuer.That's not so expensive.
Das ist ein bisschen teuer.That's a bit expensive.
Das ist ziemlich teuer.That's quite / rather expensive.
Das ist sehr teuer.That's very expensive.
Das ist zu teuer.That's too expensive. (so I won't buy it)

zu (too) is always negative. Die Jacke ist sehr schön is praise; Die Jacke ist zu groß means it doesn't fit.

Colours: Farben

rot (red), blau (blue), gelb (yellow), grün (green), schwarz (black), weiß (white), grau (grey), braun (brown), lila (purple), rosa (pink), orange (orange). Add hell- (light) or dunkel- (dark) in front: hellblau (light blue), dunkelgrün (dark green). Chapter 41 covers colours in full. (The topic sheet pairs colours as "opposites", like red and green; those are just pairs to learn together, not true opposites.)

Other common umlaut adjectives: arm, dumm, kalt, klug, krank, kurz, lang, oft, scharf, schwach, stark, warm. Note that groß is an exception to the -esten rule: am größten.

als and wie

  • Different: comparative + als (than): Berlin ist größer als München. (Berlin is bigger than Munich.)
  • The same: so + adjective + wie (as ... as): Anna ist so alt wie Tom. (Anna is as old as Tom.)
  • Not the same: nicht so ... wie: Der Bus ist nicht so schnell wie der Zug. (The bus isn't as fast as the train.)

Three comparison mistakes

  • "mehr schön" copies English "more beautiful". German always uses the ending, even for long words: schöner, interessanter.
  • "größer wie" is heard in some dialects but is wrong in standard German: größer als.
  • gern has its own forms: Ich trinke gern Tee, lieber Kaffee, und am liebsten Saft. (I like tea, prefer coffee, and like juice best.)

4. Adjectives in front of a noun: endings in all four cases

When an adjective stands directly before a noun, it takes an ending: der große Hund (the big dog), ein großer Hund (a big dog). This is the part learners find hardest, so here is the logic first, then the complete tables. You need the case system for this (Chapter 10), so it helps to come back here after Chapters 10–12. At A1 the exam mostly expects the nominative and accusative; the dative and genitive rows complete the picture.

The logic behind all the endings

German wants the gender and case to be shown once, clearly. If the article already shows it clearly (der, dem, des), the adjective gets a simple ending, -e or -en. If the article doesn't show it (ein could be masculine or neuter), or there is no article at all, the adjective takes over and shows it, with endings that look like der / das: ein großer (like der), ein großes (like das).

Table 1: after der / die / das (also dieser, welcher, jeder)

Here the adjective carries almost exactly the endings of der / die / das itself (dem → kaltem, der → kalter). You'll see these on menus and in adverts: frischer Fisch (fresh fish), mit heißem Wasser (with hot water).

Two things that never take endings

  • Adjectives after sein: Das Haus ist groß, never "Das Haus ist großes."
  • The colours lila, rosa, orange officially stay unchanged even before a noun: ein rosa Kleid (a pink dress).

A superlative before a noun takes the same endings: Das ist der schönste Tag. (That's the most beautiful day.) · Sie wohnt in der größten Stadt. (She lives in the biggest city.)

5. Adjectives at work

Wohnungsanzeigen (flat adverts)

A) Schöne, helle 2-Zimmer-Wohnung, 55 m², ruhige Lage, nah am Bahnhof. 690 € warm.
B) Große 3-Zimmer-Wohnung mit neuem Bad und kleinem Balkon, 70 m², leider etwas laut. 820 € warm.

(A: Beautiful, bright 2-room flat, 55 m², quiet location, close to the station. 690 euros including heating. B: Large 3-room flat with a new bathroom and a small balcony, 70 m², unfortunately a bit noisy. 820 euros including heating.)

Wohnung B ist größer und teurer als Wohnung A. Wohnung A ist ruhiger. Für mich ist Wohnung A am besten, denn sie ist günstiger und nah am Bahnhof. (Flat B is bigger and more expensive than flat A. Flat A is quieter. For me flat A is best, because it's cheaper and close to the station.)
