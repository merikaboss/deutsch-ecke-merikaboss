Adjektive und Gegenteile

Adjectives and their opposites

Original topic sheet View "Adjektive und Gegenteile" in the Picture Book →

📐 Good news first: adjectives after "sein" never change

When an adjective comes after the verb sein (to be), describing the subject, it stays in its plain dictionary form no matter the gender or number of the noun. This is different from adjectives placed directly before a noun, which take endings you'll learn later (once the case system from Chapters 10–19 is in place) — for now, focus on this easier, more common pattern.

💬 Examples

  • Der Tisch ist groß. (The table is big.)
  • Die Tasche ist klein. (The bag is small.)
  • Das Wetter ist schön. (The weather is beautiful.)
  • Die Bücher sind teuer. (The books are expensive.)

No -e, -er, -es ending added anywhere — same word, every time.

Asking about a quality with "Wie"

💬 Examples

  • Wie ist das Wetter? (How's the weather?) — Es ist kalt. (It's cold.)
  • Wie ist der Film? (How is the movie?) — Er ist langweilig. (It's boring.)

⚠️ Common mistake: adding endings too early

Once learners find out German adjectives sometimes take endings (ein großes Haus), there's a temptation to add endings everywhere, including after sein. Resist it — after sein, the adjective never changes: always Das Haus ist groß, never "Das Haus ist großes."

Exercises

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Give the opposite of: groß, teuer, schnell, sauber.
2. Translate: "The coffee is hot. The tea is cold."
3. Answer in German: Wie ist dein Zimmer (room)? — describe it with at least two adjectives.
4. Spot and fix the mistake: "Die Suppe ist heißes."
5. Translate: "Is the exercise easy or difficult?"
