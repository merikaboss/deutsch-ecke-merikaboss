Zahlen

Numbers: 0–20, 21–100, and above 100

0 to 12: just memorize these

Like English, German numbers 0–12 don't follow a pattern — you simply have to learn each one.

13 to 19: a clear pattern

From 13 onward, German builds the number as [digit] + zehn ([digit] + "-teen") — very close to English:

13dreizehn17siebzehn (drops the -en from sieben)
14vierzehn18achtzehn
15fünfzehn19neunzehn
16sechzehn (drops the -s from sechs)

📐 Grammar rule: numbers 21–99 are built backwards from English

To make 21–99, German says the units digit first, then und (and), then the tens — the opposite order from English. All three parts are written as one word.

⚠️ Common mistake: reading long numbers left to right

Because English speakers read digits left to right, there's a strong urge to say "zwanzig-ein" for 21. That's wrong and won't be understood as a normal number — the units digit always comes first: einundzwanzig, never "zwanzigeins". This backwards order is one of the most-cited hard parts of German for English speakers — the only fix is repetition until it's automatic.

📐 Grammar rule: numbers over 100 are still one long word

German doesn't put spaces or hyphens between the parts of a number, no matter how long it gets: dreihundertfünfundsechzig (three hundred and sixty-five, 365) is written as a single word. Reading long numbers out loud takes practice — break them into hundreds / tens-and-units chunks in your head as you read.

A quick note on "eins" vs "ein"

The number "one" is eins when counting on its own (eins, zwei, drei...) but becomes ein (and later changes its ending depending on gender and case — covered in Chapter 11) when it comes directly before a noun: ein Buch (one book / a book), not "eins Buch."

Exercises

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Write out the German word for: 7, 14, 30, 16.
2. Write out the German word for 21, 58, and 99, following the "units-and-tens" pattern.
3. What's wrong with saying "zwanzigsieben" for 27? Write the correct word.
4. Write out the German word for 100, 365, and 1.000.
5. Translate into German (as digits are fine, but say the words in your head): "I have two brothers and one sister."
6. Your phone number is 0176 4 2 8 9. Practice saying each digit aloud in German.
