Ordnungszahlen

Ordinal numbers: first, second, third...

Original topic sheet View "Deutsche Ordnungszahlen" in the Picture Book →

Grammar rule: two different endings, split at the number 20

Ordinal numbers 1–19 are built by adding -te to the cardinal number (with a handful of irregulars). From 20 onward, the ending is -ste instead.

CardinalOrdinalEnglish
einsder erste (irregular)first
zweider zweitesecond
dreider dritte (irregular)third
vierder viertefourth
fünfder fünftefifth
sechsder sechstesixth
siebender siebte (drops the -en)seventh
achtder achteeighth
neunder neunteninth
zehnder zehntetenth
zwanzigder zwanzigstetwentieth
einundzwanzigder einundzwanzigstetwenty-first
hundertder hundertstehundredth

Examples in context

  • Das ist mein erster Deutschkurs. (This is my first German course.)
  • Sie wohnt im dritten Stock. (She lives on the third floor.)
  • Heute ist der zwölfte Mai. (Today is the twelfth of May — see Chapter 44 for full dates.)

Common mistake: forgetting the ending changes with case and gender

Ordinal numbers act exactly like regular adjectives once attached to a noun — that means they also take an ending depending on the noun's gender and case, just like the adjectives you'll refine further in later study: der erste Tag (the first day) but am ersten Tag (on the first day — dative). Don't expect "erste" to stay frozen in every sentence.

Exercises

Try it yourself

1. Write the ordinal form of: 4, 7, 10, 21.
2. Which two ordinal numbers under 10 are irregular, and what are they?
3. At what number does the ordinal ending change from -te to -ste?
4. Translate: "This is my second book."
5. Translate: "He lives on the fifth floor."
