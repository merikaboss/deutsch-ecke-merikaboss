Haben und Sein / Regelmäßige Verben

Conjugating "to have," "to be," and regular verbs in the present tense

Original topic sheet View "Verben Konjugation" in the Picture Book →

The two most important verbs: haben and sein

haben (to have) and sein (to be) are both irregular, used constantly, and worth memorizing perfectly before anything else.

Pronounhaben (to have)sein (to be)
ichhabebin
duhastbist
er/sie/eshatist
wirhabensind
ihrhabtseid
sie/Siehabensind

💬 Examples

  • Ich habe einen Bruder. (I have a brother.)
  • Wir sind müde. (We are tired.)
  • Hast du Zeit? (Do you have time?)

Regular verbs: the standard pattern

Most German verbs are "regular" (also called weak verbs) — they follow one predictable pattern. Take the infinitive (the "to ___" form, always ending in -en), remove -en to get the stem, and add the ending that matches the subject.

📐 Grammar rule: the regular verb endings

PronounEndinglernen (to learn)wohnen (to live)
ich-elernewohne
du-stlernstwohnst
er/sie/es-tlerntwohnt
wir-enlernenwohnen
ihr-tlerntwohnt
sie/Sie-enlernenwohnen

A small spelling adjustment: verbs ending in -t or -d

If the stem ends in -t or -d (like arbeiten (to work)), an extra -e- is inserted before the du, er/sie/es, and ihr endings, purely so the word stays pronounceable.

Pronounarbeiten (to work)
icharbeite
duarbeitest
er/sie/esarbeitet
ihrarbeitet

Another adjustment: verbs ending in -s, -ß, -z, -x

If the stem already ends in an s-like sound (tanzen (to dance), heißen (to be called)), the du-form drops the extra -s to avoid a triple-s:

Pronountanzen (to dance)heißen (to be called)
ichtanzeheiße
dutanzt (not "tanzst")heißt (not "heißst")

⚠️ Common mistake: forgetting the ending changes at all

English barely changes its verbs ("I learn, you learn, we learn" — only "he/she/it learns" is different). It's tempting to treat German the same way and just use one form for everyone. Every single subject pronoun needs its own ending in German — there's no shortcut.

Exercises

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Conjugate sein for wir and for ihr.
2. Conjugate haben for du and for sie (she).
3. Conjugate the regular verb machen (to do/make) for all six pronouns.
4. Conjugate warten (to wait) for du — remember the spelling adjustment for stems ending in -t.
5. Conjugate heißen for du — remember the spelling adjustment for stems ending in an s-sound.
6. Translate: "I have a sister. She is 20 years old. We live in Berlin."
