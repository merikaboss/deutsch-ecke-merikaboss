Fragewort „welch-" (Übersicht)

Asking "which?" — what welch- means, how it takes der/die/das endings, its forms in all four cases, and how to answer

Original topic sheet View "Fragepronomen – Welcher Welche Welches (Overview)" in the Picture Book →

When there are several options and you want someone to pick one, you ask which?: which bus, which colour, which day. German uses welch- with an ending — welcher, welche, welches, welchen, welchem. It looks like a lot of forms, but there is nothing new to learn: the endings are exactly the endings of der / die / das. If you know the definite article in a case (Chapter 10), you already know welch- in that case. This chapter explains the meaning and the system; Chapter 36 practises every case in detail.

1. What welch- asks

A choice from a known set

welch- asks the listener to select one or more items from a group that both of you have in mind:

Welcher Bus fährt zum Bahnhof? (Which bus goes to the station? — there are several buses)
Welche Farbe magst du? (Which colour do you like?)
Welches Zimmer ist frei? (Which room is free?)

Grammatically, welch- before a noun is a Frageartikel (question article) — it takes the place of der/die/das. Used on its own, without a noun, it's a Fragepronomen (question pronoun): Welcher gefällt dir? (Which one do you like?)

2. The core idea: copy the article's last letter

der → welcher · die → welche · das → welches

ArticleGender / numberwelch-Example
dermasculinewelcherWelcher Bruder kommt? (Which brother is coming?)
diefemininewelcheWelche Schwester kommt mit? (Which sister is coming along?)
dasneuterwelchesWelches Buch liest du? (Which book are you reading?)
diepluralwelcheWelche Freunde sind da? (Which friends are here?)

Think of it as welch- + the article's ending: der, die, das (-es, because welchs can't be pronounced), dem, den. This works in every case.

3. All four cases in one table

welch- next to der/die/das

CaseMasculineFeminineNeuterPlural
Nominativder → welcherdie → welchedas → welchesdie → welche
Akkusativden → welchendie → welchedas → welchesdie → welche
Dativdem → welchemder → welcherdem → welchemden → welchen (+ n)
Genitivdes → welches (+ s)der → welcherdes → welches (+ s)der → welcher

Only the masculine changes between Nominativ and Akkusativ, just like der → den. The dative plural noun takes an extra -n (mit welchen Freunden), and masculine/neuter nouns take -s in the Genitiv (welches Landes). The Genitiv form is rare in speech; you'll recognise it in written texts.

One example per case

CaseQuestionEnglish
Nominativ (subject)Welcher Zug fährt nach Berlin?Which train goes to Berlin?
Akkusativ (object)Welchen Zug nimmst du?Which train are you taking?
Dativ (after mit)Mit welchem Zug fährst du?Which train are you travelling on?
GenitivDie Adresse welcher Firma brauchen Sie?Which company's address do you need? (written style)

4. How to choose the right form — three steps

Gender → case → ending

  1. Find the noun's article. Tasche → die.
  2. Find the case. Is the noun the subject (Nom), the object of the verb (Akk), after a dative preposition like mit, bei, von, aus, zu (Dat) or an accusative preposition like für (Akk)?
  3. Put the article in that case and copy its ending. mit + die Tasche → mit der Tasche → mit welcher Tasche?

Worked example: "Which film are you watching?" — der Film; you watch it = object = Akkusativ → den Film → Welchen Film siehst du?

5. Word order

welch- + noun = position 1

The whole phrase welch- + noun counts as one block in position 1, then comes the verb:

Position 1VerbRest
Welche Sprachensprichstdu?
In welcher StraßewohnenSie?
Um welche Uhrzeitbeginntder Kurs?

A preposition always stays in front of welch-: Aus welchem Land kommst du? — never „Welchem Land kommst du aus?"

6. Answering a welch- question

Answer with the definite article (or dies-)

Because welch- asks for a specific item, the answer uses der/die/das, a demonstrative (Chapter 37), or a description — in the same case as the question:

Welchen Pullover nimmst du? — Den blauen. / Den hier. (The blue one. / This one.)
Welche Tasche gehört dir? — Die schwarze. (The black one.)
Mit welchem Bus fahren wir? — Mit dem Bus Nummer 7.
Welches Kleid gefällt dir? — Das rote.

Short answers like den blauen use an adjective ending — you'll recognise these from Chapter 41 (colours).

7. welch- without a noun

"Which one?"

When the noun is already clear, drop it and keep the ending:
Ich hätte gern einen Kuchen. — Welchen? (I'd like a cake. — Which one?)
Da sind zwei Jacken. Welche ist deine? (There are two jackets. Which one is yours?)
Hier sind drei Zimmer. Welches möchten Sie?

In spoken German, welche also means "some" when referring back to an uncountable or plural noun: Hast du Brot? — Ja, ich habe welches. (Yes, I have some.) This is a different use — it doesn't ask anything.

8. welch- vs was vs was für ein vs wer

Four question words, four jobs

WordAsksExampleTypical answer
waswhat? (open)Was trinkst du?Tee.
welch-which one from a set?Welchen Tee trinkst du?Den grünen.
was für einwhat kind of?Was für einen Tee trinkst du?Einen grünen Tee.
werwho? (person, no noun)Wer kommt?Maria.

The difference between welch- and was für ein shows in the answer: welch- is answered with der/die/das (a specific item), was für ein with ein/eine (a type). In a shop with three cars in the window: Welches Auto möchten Sie? In general conversation: Was für ein Auto hast du? — Ein kleines.

Corrections to the topic sheet

  • „Nach Präpositionen wie mit, bei, von steht oft die 4. Form: welchen" is wrong. mit, bei, von take the Dativ, so the form is welchem (masculine/neuter), welcher (feminine) or welchen (plural only): mit welchem Bus, bei welcher Firma, von welchen Freunden. welchen in the sheet's Welchen Film siehst du? is correct, but because it's masculine Akkusativ.
  • „Die Endung -e bleibt gleich bei allen Formen" is misleading. The endings are -er, -e, -es, -en, -em and they change with gender and case. What stays the same is the stem welch-.
  • The four forms listed are only the Nominativ. The full system has all four cases (section 3).
  • The box "Fragen mit wann / wo / warum" doesn't actually combine those words. welch- can replace them with a more precise question: wann → An welchem Tag?, wo → In welcher Stadt?, warum → Aus welchem Grund?

Typical learner mistakes

WrongRightWhy
Welcher Film siehst du?Welchen Film siehst du?The film is the object → Akkusativ.

9. Everyday questions with welch-

Questions you'll hear and ask

  • Welcher Tag ist heute? (What day is it today?)
  • Welche Sprachen sprechen Sie? (Which languages do you speak?)
  • In welchem Stock wohnst du? (Which floor do you live on?)
  • Welche Größe haben Sie? (What size are you?)
  • Welche Hausnummer? (Which house number?)
  • Von welchem Gleis fährt der Zug ab? (Which platform does the train leave from?)
  • Welchen Beruf haben Sie? (What's your job?)
  • Bei welcher Firma arbeitest du? (Which company do you work for?)
  • Welche Telefonnummer haben Sie? (What's your phone number?)

Notice that English often says "what" where German says welch-: size, day, number and job are chosen from a fixed set, so German uses welch-.

Mini-Dialog: in a bookshop

A: Ich suche ein Buch für meinen Sohn.
B: Welches Buch suchen Sie? Haben Sie einen Titel?
A: Nein. Er ist zehn und liest gern Abenteuergeschichten.
B: Hier sind zwei. Welches finden Sie besser?
A: Das mit dem Hund. In welcher Sprache ist das andere?
B: Auf Englisch. Welche Sprache spricht Ihr Sohn?
A: Deutsch und Englisch. Dann nehme ich beide!

(I'm looking for a book for my son. — Which book are you looking for? Do you have a title? — No. He's ten and likes adventure stories. — Here are two. Which do you think is better? — The one with the dog. What language is the other one in? — English. Which language does your son speak? — German and English. Then I'll take both!)
