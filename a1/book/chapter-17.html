W-Fragen und Fragewörter der Zeit

Question words, and asking about time

Original topic sheet View "W-Fragen und Zeitfragen" in the Picture Book →

These are called W-Fragen because nearly every one starts with the letter W. You already used two of them (wie, woher) back in Chapter 1 — here's the complete set, and the reminder that verb-second (Chapter 5) still applies after every one of them.

GermanEnglish
werwho
waswhat
wowhere (at)
wohinwhere to
woherwhere from
wannwhen
warumwhy
wiehow
wie vielhow much
wie vielehow many
welche(r/s)which (full detail in Chapter 35)

📐 Grammar rule: wo vs. wohin vs. woher

English uses "where" for all three, but German distinguishes location from direction: wo asks where something is (static), wohin asks where something is going to, and woher asks where something is coming from.

💬 Examples

  • Wo wohnst du? (Where do you live?) — Ich wohne in Berlin.
  • Wohin gehst du? (Where are you going?) — Ich gehe nach Hause.
  • Woher kommst du? (Where are you from?) — Ich komme aus Uganda.
  • Wer ist das? (Who is that?) — Das ist mein Bruder.
  • Wie viele Geschwister hast du? (How many siblings do you have?)

Asking about time specifically

GermanEnglishExample answer
Wann?When?Um sieben Uhr. (At seven o'clock.)
Wie lange?How long?Zwei Stunden. (Two hours.)
Wie oft?How often?Jeden Tag. (Every day.)
Seit wann?Since when?Seit letztem Jahr. (Since last year.)

⚠️ Common mistake: "was" instead of "wer" for people

was only asks about things, never people. To ask "who is that," it's always Wer ist das?, never "Was ist das?" (which would ask what an object is).

Exercises

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Fill in wo, wohin, or woher: ___ fährst du? (asking where someone is headed)
2. Ask "Why are you learning German?" in German.
3. Ask "How many books do you have?" in German.
4. Translate: "When does the train leave?"
5. Which question word is wrong here, and why: "Was ist dein Bruder?" when you mean "Who is your brother?"
