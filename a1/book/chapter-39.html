Formular ausfüllen

Every field on a German form, how to write dates, addresses and nationalities, the instructions forms give you, spoken form interviews, and the A1 exam form task

Original topic sheet View "Formular ausfuellen" in the Picture Book →

Germany runs on forms: registering your address, joining a language course, opening a bank account, checking in at a hotel, visiting a new doctor, getting a library card. The good news is that forms use a small, fixed vocabulary, and once you know it you can fill in almost anything. It is also Part 1 of the A1 writing exam: you read a short text about a person and complete a form for them. This chapter gives you every common field, the formats Germans expect, the instruction words, and plenty of practice.

3. Formats Germans expect

Dates: day · month · year

TT.MM.JJJJ = Tag.Monat.Jahr (day.month.year), separated by full stops. 05.12.2025 is 5 December, not 12 May. In letters, write 5. Dezember 2025 — the full stop after the number shows it's an ordinal.

Reading a date aloud: 03.11.1998 → der dritte elfte neunzehnhundertachtundneunzig or der dritte November neunzehnhundertachtundneunzig. Years up to 1999 are read in hundreds (neunzehnhundert...); from 2000 as thousands (zweitausendfünf, zweitausendsechsundzwanzig). "I was born on 3 November" = Ich bin am dritten November geboren. (See Chapter 44 for dates.)

Addresses, phone numbers, emails

  • Street first, number after: Bahnhofstraße 10, not 10 Bahnhofstraße. Flats: Bahnhofstraße 10, 3. OG (Obergeschoss, floor).
  • Postcode before the town: 12345 Berlin.
  • Phone numbers are usually read in pairs or single digits: 0176 – null eins sieben sechs, then zwölf – vierunddreißig – sechsundfünfzig – achtundsiebzig. International: +49 (Germany) replaces the first 0.
  • Emails aloud: @ = "ät", . = Punkt, - = Bindestrich, _ = Unterstrich. s.okello@mail.de = "s Punkt okello ät mail Punkt de".
  • Names: if asked Wie schreibt man das?, spell with the German alphabet (Chapter 3): O – K – E – L – L – O, or O – K – E – Doppel-L – O.

Fixed sentences on forms

  • Hiermit melde ich mich verbindlich zum Kurs an. (I hereby register for the course — binding.)
  • Hiermit bestätige ich, dass meine Angaben richtig und vollständig sind. (I hereby confirm that my information is correct and complete.)
  • Ich bin mit den Teilnahmebedingungen einverstanden. (I agree to the terms of participation.)
  • Ich stimme der Verarbeitung meiner Daten zu. (I agree to the processing of my data.)

Dialog: in the library

A: Guten Tag, ich möchte einen Bibliotheksausweis.
B: Gern. Dann füllen Sie bitte dieses Formular aus. Haben Sie Ihren Ausweis dabei?
A: Ja, hier ist mein Pass. Muss ich alles ausfüllen?
B: Nur die Felder mit Stern. Die Telefonnummer ist freiwillig.
A: Was bedeutet „Nichtzutreffendes streichen"?
B: Sie streichen durch, was nicht für Sie gilt. Und unten bitte unterschreiben.
A: Hier, bitte. Ist alles richtig?
B: Ja, perfekt. Ihr Ausweis kostet 12 Euro im Jahr.

(I'd like a library card. — Please fill in this form. Do you have your ID with you? — Yes, here's my passport. Do I have to fill in everything? — Only the fields with a star. The phone number is optional. — What does "cross out what doesn't apply" mean? — You cross out what doesn't apply to you. And please sign at the bottom. — Here you are. Is everything right? — Yes, perfect. Your card costs 12 euros a year.)

Corrections to the topic sheet

  • „Nationalität: Deutsch": on a form the nationality is an adjective with a small letter — deutsch. Deutsch with a capital is the language.
  • „der Name (name)": on German forms Name alone means the surname, not your full name. The first name goes under Vorname.
  • „Hiermit bestätige ich die Richtigkeit." is incomplete — the correctness of what? Full phrase: Hiermit bestätige ich die Richtigkeit meiner Angaben.
  • The date boxes show TT.MM.JJJ with three year boxes in the filled example — years always have four digits: JJJJ.
  • „PLZ + Ort: 12345 Berlin" is correct; note that German postcodes can start with 0 (01067 Dresden) — never drop the zero.

Typical learner mistakes

  • Writing the date the English way: 12/05/2005 or 05.12.2005 for 12 May → 12.05.2005.
  • Putting the full name under Name, then repeating the first name under Vorname.
  • Writing a sentence in a field: „Ich bin ledig" → just ledig.
  • Forgetting Ort, Datum next to the signature — forms without it are often returned.
  • „Ich bin geboren am 1996" → Ich bin 1996 geboren. (years without am) / Ich bin am 14. Februar 1996 geboren.

7. The A1 exam: Schreiben Teil 1

How the task works

You read a short text about a person — often someone registering for something — and fill in five missing fields in a form with single words or numbers. Strategy: read the form first, so you know what to look for; underline the information in the text; copy spellings exactly; write dates as TT.MM.JJJJ; watch out for distractors (the text may mention two dates or two addresses).
