Grammar rule: German dates go day.month.year, not month/day/year

A German date written 05.12.2025 means the 5th of December, not May 12th. This trips up English speakers constantly on real forms — always read German dates as day first.

Common mistake: mixing up Vorname and Name

On a German form, Name (or Nachname) means surname/family name, and Vorname means first name — the reverse order from how English "name" usually gets used casually. Filling "Anna" under Name instead of Vorname is an easy real-world slip.

Übungen (Exercises)

Try it yourself

1. Wie liest man dieses Datum: 03.11.1998? (Read the date out loud — day first!)
2. Fill in this mini-form about yourself, in German: Name: ___ Vorname: ___ Geburtsdatum: ___ Nationalität: ___ Beruf: ___
3. Übersetzen Sie: "I was born on the 5th of March. I work as a teacher."
4. Was ist der Unterschied zwischen "Name" und "Vorname" auf einem deutschen Formular? (Explain the difference.)
5. Write a full sentence, in German, confirming that the information you provided on a form is correct (use the fixed phrase from this chapter).
