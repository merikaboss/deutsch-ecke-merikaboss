Das Datum

Dates, precisely: "today is the..." vs. "on the..."

Original topic sheet View "Am + Datum" in the Picture Book →

Chapter 43 gave you the basic date pattern. This chapter makes it precise — there are actually two different forms depending on what you're saying, and mixing them up is an easy, common mistake.

📐 Grammar rule: identifying a date vs. saying something happens ON it

To simply identify what day it is, use der + ordinal (nominative, from Chapter 20): Heute ist der 14. Mai. To say something happens on that date, switch to am + ordinal with a dative -en ending: Mein Geburtstag ist am 14. Mai — spoken as am vierzehnten Mai.

Ordinal (nominative, Ch. 20)After "am" (dative)
der ersteam ersten
der fünfteam fünften
der zehnteam zehnten
der zwanzigsteam zwanzigsten
der einundzwanzigsteam einundzwanzigsten

Notice the pattern from Chapter 20 still holds: 1st–19th end in -ten, 20th onward in -sten — just with the extra dative -n already built into that ending, so there's actually nothing new to add.

💬 Beispiele

  • Mein Geburtstag ist am fünften März. (My birthday is on the 5th of March.)
  • Wir treffen uns am siebzehnten Juni. (We're meeting on the 17th of June.)
  • Die Prüfung ist am zwanzigsten Dezember. (The exam is on the 20th of December.)

📐 am for dates, an for weekdays — both fuse "an + dem"

Both am fünften Mai and am Montag (Chapter 43) use the same fused preposition am = "an + dem" (Chapter 24's location prepositions take dative too) — one consistent rule quietly running underneath both date and weekday expressions.

⚠️ Common mistake: using the nominative ordinal after "am"

"am fünfte März" looks reasonable but is wrong — after am, the ordinal must take the dative ending: am fünften März.

Übungen (Exercises)

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Ergänzen Sie: Heute ist d__ 3. Januar. Wir treffen uns am 3___ Januar. (fill in both the article/ordinal ending — notice they differ)
2. Übersetzen Sie: "My birthday is on the 21st of September."
3. Fix the mistake: "Die Party ist am zehnte Mai."
4. Beantworten Sie auf Deutsch, mit "am": Wann ist dein Geburtstag?
5. Write three sentences about upcoming plans, each with a different date using "am + ordinal."
