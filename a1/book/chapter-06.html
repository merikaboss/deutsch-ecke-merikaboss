Personalpronomen (ich, du, wir...)

Subject pronouns: who's doing the action

Original topic sheet View "Personalpronomen (1., 2., 3. Person)" in the Picture Book →

These are the pronouns that go in the subject slot of a sentence — the "who" that's doing the action. This is the nominative (subject) case; you'll meet the same pronouns wearing different clothes in the accusative and dative cases in Chapter 19.

📐 Grammar rule: "sie" has three different jobs

The single spelling sie covers she, they, and — capitalized as Sie — the formal you. Context and capitalization are the only way to tell them apart in writing; in speech, verb conjugation and context do the job.

💬 Examples: er / sie / es matching noun gender

  • Der Tisch ist groß. Er ist braun. (The table is big. It [masculine] is brown.)
  • Die Lampe ist alt. Sie ist kaputt. (The lamp is old. It [feminine] is broken.)
  • Das Auto ist neu. Es ist schnell. (The car is new. It [neuter] is fast.)

⚠️ Common mistake: using "es" for every "it"

English has one word for "it," so English speakers default to es for everything. But German objects have grammatical gender (der/die/das — covered in Chapter 10), and the pronoun must match: a der-word gets er, a die-word gets sie, even though both just mean "it" in English.

du, ihr, or Sie? Choosing the right "you"

InformalFormal
One personduSie
More than one personihrSie

When unsure whether someone expects du or Sie, default to Sie — it's always safe to be too formal, never safe to be too casual with a stranger.

Exercises

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Which pronoun replaces der Stuhl (the chair)? Which replaces die Tür (the door)? Which replaces das Fenster (the window)?
2. You're talking to your grandmother. Which "you" do you use — du, ihr, or Sie?
3. You're talking to three of your close friends at once. Which "you" do you use?
4. Explain the three different meanings of the word sie.
5. Translate: "She is nice. He is funny. They are students."
