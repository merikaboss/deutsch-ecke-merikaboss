Zeitadverbien

Adverbs of time: when, how often, in what order

Original topic sheet View "Zeitadverbien" in the Picture Book →

Good news for a change: Zeitadverbien (adverbs of time) never change form — no gender, no case, no conjugation. The only real skill is knowing where they go in the sentence.

📐 Grammar rule: three valid positions, one rule that never bends

A time adverb can go at the start (position 1), right after the verb, or at the end — but wherever it lands, Chapter 5's verb-second rule still governs the sentence, and the subject still has to move after the verb if the time word takes position 1.

💬 Same sentence, three valid word orders

  • Morgen gehe ich zur Schule. (Tomorrow I'm going to school.)
  • Ich gehe morgen zur Schule.
  • Ich gehe zur Schule morgen. (less common, but not wrong)

⚠️ Common mistake: adding an ending to a time adverb

Since so much German grammar involves endings changing by gender and case, learners sometimes instinctively try to modify oft or immer. Don't — these words are frozen; they never change no matter what they're describing.

Übungen (Exercises)

✏️ Try it yourself

1. Ergänzen Sie: Ich komme ___ (morgens) zur Schule. Wir treffen uns ___ (am Samstag).
2. Sortieren Sie nach Häufigkeit, von "nie" bis "immer": oft, selten, manchmal, immer, nie.
3. Übersetzen Sie, mit dem Zeitadverb am Satzanfang: "Tomorrow I am learning German."
4. Fix the word order: "Morgen ich gehe ins Kino."
5. Write four sentences describing your typical week, using zuerst, dann, danach, and schließlich to sequence your activities.
