Der, die oder das?

How to work out a noun's gender — the ending rules, the meaning groups, the exceptions to watch — and over 600 everyday nouns sorted by der, die and das

Why is it der Kugelschreiber (the pen) but das Buch (the book) and die Tasche (the bag)? Every learner asks this, and it is the question that slows down speaking more than any other. Chapter 10 introduced the three genders and a first set of patterns. This chapter goes all the way: it shows you how to work out the article of a noun you have never seen, which rules you can trust completely, which exceptions actually come up at A1, and what to do in the moment when you are not sure. The second half is a large word bank — over 600 everyday nouns, sorted by topic and by gender, so the articles start to feel familiar instead of random.

1. The honest truth first

What really decides the gender

Gender belongs to the word, not to the thing. A table is not male and a bag is not female. But the choice is also not pure chance. Three things point to the article, in this order of strength:

ClueHow reliableExample
1. The ending of the wordthe strongest clue — several endings are right every single time-ung → always die: die Wohnung (flat)
2. The meaning groupstrong, with a few well-known exceptionsdays → der: der Montag
3. People and animalsnatural sex usually wins — unless an ending overrules itder Onkel, die Tante — but das Mädchen

No rule covers every noun. What the rules do is shrink the list of words you must simply memorise from "all of them" to "the ones without a clear clue". Those you learn together with their article — Section 9 shows how.

Don't borrow the gender from another language

If your first language has genders too — French, Spanish, Portuguese, Arabic — do not carry them over. They rarely match. French la table is der Tisch; Spanish el sol is die Sonne (the sun); Spanish la luna is der Mond (the moon). English has no genders at all, so English speakers start from zero — which is actually an advantage: nothing to unlearn.

2. The five-step check

When you meet a new noun, run through these five questions. You will usually have the answer by step 2.

Der, die oder das? — in five steps

  1. Is it a compound noun? Look only at the last part. das Haus + die Tür = die Haustür (front door). (Section 6)
  2. Does it have a telling ending? -ung, -heit, -keit, -chen and the other endings in Sections 3–5 give you the article straight away.
  3. Is it a person or an animal? Male → usually der, female → usually die. (Section 7)
  4. Does it belong to a meaning group? Days, months, weather, drinks, metals… (Section 5)
  5. Still no answer? If it ends in -e, guess die — you will be right about nine times in ten. If it is a short one-syllable word, der is the most frequent — but check it and learn it.

The check in action

Handtasche (handbag) — compound, last part is Tasche, which ends in -e → die Handtasche.
Rechnung (bill) — ending -ung → die Rechnung, no doubt at all.
Kugelschreiber (ballpoint pen) — compound, last part Schreiber ("writer"): a thing that does something, ending -er → der Kugelschreiber.
Brötchen (bread roll) — ending -chen → das Brötchen.
Buch (book) — one syllable, no ending, no group → no clue. This is one to learn: das Buch.

3. Endings that mean die

Start here, because the feminine endings are the most reliable of all. The first six are right every time — you will not find an exception at A1 level.

Always die

  • -ung (as a real ending — short words like der Sprung, jump, don't count) — die Wohnung (flat), die Zeitung (newspaper), die Übung (exercise), die Rechnung (bill)
  • -heit — die Gesundheit (health), die Freiheit (freedom), die Krankheit (illness)
  • -keit — die Möglichkeit (possibility), die Süßigkeit (sweet), die Sehenswürdigkeit (sight, attraction)
  • -schaft — die Freundschaft (friendship), die Mannschaft (team), die Landschaft (landscape)
  • -ität — die Universität (university), die Qualität (quality), die Nationalität (nationality)
  • -kunft, -fahrt — die Ankunft (arrival), die Zukunft (future), die Abfahrt (departure)

Almost always die — learn the one or two exceptions

  • -ion — die Information, die Lektion (lesson), die Region
    Watch out: das Stadion (stadium)
  • -ik — die Musik, die Grammatik, die Fabrik (factory), die Politik
    Watch out: der Katholik — a man, so der
  • -ie — die Familie, die Energie, die Biologie, die Fotografie
    Watch out: das Knie (knee), das Genie (genius)
  • -ei — die Bäckerei (bakery), die Polizei (police), die Metzgerei (butcher's)
    Watch out: das Ei (egg), der Papagei (parrot)
  • -ur — die Natur, die Kultur, die Temperatur, die Reparatur (repair)
    Watch out: das Abitur (school-leaving exam)
  • -enz, -anz — die Konferenz, die Distanz (distance), die Toleranz
  • -in (women) — die Lehrerin (female teacher), die Freundin, die Ärztin (female doctor)
    Watch out: only for female people: der Termin (appointment), das Benzin (petrol)

The big one: -e is usually die

About nine out of ten nouns ending in -e are feminine: die Lampe (lamp), die Tasche (bag), die Straße (street), die Blume (flower), die Woche (week), die Banane, die Tomate. Because so many words end in -e, this single rule is worth more than any other.

The exceptions fall into three groups you can learn:

  • Male people and animals stay der: der Junge (boy), der Kollege (colleague), der Kunde (customer), der Löwe (lion), der Affe (monkey), der Hase (hare)
  • A handful of masculine words: der Name (name), der Käse (cheese), der Gedanke (thought)
  • Neuter words, many starting with Ge-: das Auge (eye), das Ende (end), das Interesse (interest), das Gemüse (vegetables), das Gebäude (building)

4. Endings that mean das

Always das

FormExamples
-chendas Mädchen (girl), das Brötchen (bread roll), das Hähnchen (chicken), das Kaninchen (rabbit)
-leindas Fräulein (young lady, old-fashioned), das Büchlein (little book)
a verb used as a noundas Essen (food; eating), das Leben (life), das Schwimmen (swimming), das Lernen (learning)

-chen and -lein make a "small" version of any noun, and they overrule everything — even natural sex. That is why the girl is das Mädchen. It is also why die Hand becomes das Händchen (little hand).

Almost always das — learn the exceptions

  • -ment — das Dokument, das Medikament (medicine), das Instrument
    Watch out: der Moment (moment) — very common
  • -um — das Museum, das Zentrum (centre), das Datum (date), das Studium (university studies)
    Watch out: der Irrtum (mistake), der Reichtum (wealth)
  • -ma — das Thema (topic), das Klima (climate), das Komma (comma)
    Watch out: die Firma (company), die Oma, die Mama
  • -o — das Auto, das Kino (cinema), das Büro (office), das Radio, das Foto
    Watch out: der Euro, der Zoo, die Disco
  • -nis — das Ergebnis (result), das Zeugnis (school report), das Erlebnis (experience)
    Watch out: die Erlaubnis (permission), die Kenntnis (knowledge)
  • starts with Ge- — das Gemüse (vegetables), das Getränk (drink), das Geschenk (present), das Gepäck (luggage), das Gesicht (face)
    Watch out: der Geschmack (taste), die Geschichte (story; history), die Gefahr (danger)

5. Endings and groups that mean der

Endings: usually der

  • -ling — der Frühling (spring), der Lehrling (apprentice), der Schmetterling (butterfly)
  • -ismus — der Tourismus (tourism), der Optimismus (optimism)
  • -ist (people) — der Tourist, der Polizist (police officer), der Journalist
    Watch out: women: die Polizistin
  • -ig, -ich — der Honig (honey), der König (king), der Essig (vinegar), der Teppich (carpet)
  • -ant, -ent (people, animals) — der Student, der Präsident, der Praktikant (intern), der Elefant
    Watch out: things are neuter: das Talent, das Element
  • -eur, -är — der Friseur (hairdresser), der Ingenieur (engineer), der Millionär
    Watch out: women: die Friseurin
  • -or — der Motor (engine), der Doktor, der Autor (author), der Humor (humour)
    Watch out: das Labor (laboratory), das Tor (gate; goal)

-er: only a doer or a tool is der

People and machines that do something, made from a verb + -er, are masculine: der Lehrer (teacher — lehren, to teach), der Fahrer (driver), der Computer, der Drucker (printer — drucken, to print), der Wecker (alarm clock — wecken, to wake), der Fernseher (TV set), and the pen from the start of this chapter, der Kugelschreiber.

But -er on its own tells you nothing. These are not doers, and they have all three genders: die Mutter, die Butter, die Nummer, das Fenster (window), das Zimmer (room), das Wasser, das Messer (knife), der Keller (cellar).

Short nouns made from a verb: usually der

When a verb loses its ending and becomes a noun, the result is usually masculine: der Anfang (beginning — anfangen), der Besuch (visit — besuchen), der Kauf (purchase — kaufen), der Schlaf (sleep — schlafen), der Anruf (phone call — anrufen), der Flug (flight — fliegen). Two common exceptions: das Spiel (game) and das Verbot (ban).

Meaning groups

These groups work because the general word behind them has that gender — every day is a der Tag, every drink with alcohol goes back to der Alkohol.

Groups that are der

  • Days, months, seasons: der Montag, der Mai, der Sommer
  • Times of day: der Morgen, der Mittag, der Abend — but die Nacht (night)
  • Weather: der Regen (rain), der Schnee (snow), der Wind, der Nebel (fog) — but das Wetter, das Gewitter (thunderstorm), die Sonne
  • Directions: der Norden, der Süden, der Osten, der Westen
  • Drinks with alcohol: der Wein (wine), der Sekt (sparkling wine), der Schnaps — but das Bier (beer)
  • Currencies: der Euro, der Dollar, der Schilling
  • Car makes: der BMW, der Toyota — because of der Wagen (car)
  • Mountains: der Kilimandscharo, der Mount Everest — because of der Berg

Groups that are die

  • Numbers as nouns: die Eins (the number one; top school mark), die Sieben — because of die Zahl
  • Most trees and flowers: die Eiche (oak), die Palme (palm), die Rose, die Tulpe — but der Baum (tree)
  • Ships and planes by name: die Titanic, die Boeing 747
  • Most German rivers: die Elbe, die Donau (Danube) — but der Rhein, and big rivers abroad are mostly masculine: der Nil

Groups that are das

  • Young beings: das Kind (child), das Baby, das Kalb (calf), das Lamm (lamb)
  • Metals and materials: das Gold, das Silber, das Eisen (iron), das Holz (wood), das Glas (glass)
  • Colours and languages as nouns: das Rot (red), das Blau, das Deutsch (the German language)
  • Letters of the alphabet: das A, das B, das Eszett
  • Fractions: das Viertel (quarter), das Drittel (third) — but die Hälfte (half)
  • Most countries and towns: das schöne Kenia, das alte Berlin — usually said without an article. The ones that need one are exceptions: die Schweiz, die Türkei, der Irak, die USA

6. Compound nouns: the last word decides

Look only at the end

German builds long nouns by joining short ones. However long the word, the last part gives the article of the whole word. The front parts only describe it. A small linking -s- or -n- in the middle changes nothing.

PartsWhole word
die Kugel + der Schreiberder Kugelschreiber (ballpoint pen)
kühl + der Schrankder Kühlschrank (fridge)
wohnen + das Zimmerdas Wohnzimmer (living room)
der Bus + die Haltestelledie Bushaltestelle (bus stop)
die Geburt + der Tagder Geburtstag (birthday)
der Fußball + das Spieldas Fußballspiel (football match)
der Apfel + der Saftder Apfelsaft (apple juice)
die Hand + die Taschedie Handtasche (handbag)
das Haus + die Aufgabedie Hausaufgabe (homework)
die Woche + das Endedas Wochenende (weekend)

This is one of the most useful tricks in the whole language: once you know der Tag, you also know der Geburtstag, der Feiertag, der Montag, der Nachmittag and every other word that ends in -tag.

7. People and animals

Natural sex usually wins

For people, the article normally follows whether the person is a man or a woman: der Mann, die Frau, der Vater, die Mutter, der Sohn, die Tochter. Most jobs have a male form and a female form with -in: der Arzt / die Ärztin (doctor), der Koch / die Köchin (cook), der Verkäufer / die Verkäuferin (shop assistant). Animals with a separate male and female word do the same: der Hahn / die Henne (rooster / hen), der Stier / die Kuh (bull / cow).

Six people-words that ignore the person

WordWhy
das Mädchen (girl)-chen is always neuter
das Kind (child), das Babyyoung beings are neuter
die Person (person)-ion → die, also for a man
der Mensch (human being)masculine, also for a woman
der Gast (guest)masculine, also for a woman
das Mitglied (member)neuter for everyone

Pronouns follow the grammar word, not the person: Das Mädchen ist neu. Es heißt Amina. (The girl is new. She is called Amina.) In everyday speech many Germans already say sie here, and both are understood.

9. When you are not sure — and how to make articles stick

In the moment: ways around the article

  • Use ein. In the Nominativ, ein works for both der and das words: Das ist ein Tisch. Das ist ein Buch. — you don't have to choose. (In the Akkusativ it becomes einen for masculine, so this only helps in the Nominativ.)
  • Use the plural. Every plural is die: Die Bücher sind neu.
  • Don't stop. A wrong article almost never stops anyone understanding you. Saying the sentence with a small mistake is far better than not saying it.

Long term: learn so it sticks

  1. Never learn a naked noun. Always article + noun + plural, as one unit: der Tisch, die Tische. On flashcards and in notebooks, write der Tisch, -e.
  2. Use colour. A method many learners use: blue for der, red for die, green for das. Underline the noun or the whole card in its colour. After a while you "see" the colour when you think of the word.
  3. Learn it in a tiny sentence. Der Tisch ist neu. Die Tasche ist schwer. Das Buch ist gut. The ear remembers a whole phrase better than a single word.
  4. Revise by ending, not alphabet. Put all your -ung words together, all your -chen words together. The rule then does the remembering for you.
  5. Check in a dictionary. Dictionaries mark the gender with a letter: m (maskulin) = der, f (feminin) = die, n (neutral) = das.
  6. Collect your own "difficult" list. The words with no clue — das Buch, der Tisch, die Hand, das Haus — go on one page that you read every few days. It is shorter than you fear.

Lösungen (Answers)

Check your work

  1. die Zeitung · das Häuschen · der Frühling · die Freiheit · das Dokument
  2. der Tourist · die Information · das Museum · die Möglichkeit · das Thema
  3. die Musik · der Honig · das Ergebnis · die Kultur · das Schwimmen
  4. der Student · die Polizei · die Mannschaft · das Tischlein · der Optimismus — -lein makes even der Tisch neuter.
  5. das Kinderzimmer · die Sonnenbrille · der Apfelbaum
  6. der Busfahrer · das Taschengeld · der Hausschuh
  7. der Geburtstagskuchen · die Telefonnummer · das Schlafzimmerfenster — only the last part counts, however long the word.
  8. der Name — one of the few masculine words in -e.
  9. der Euro — currencies are masculine.
  10. der Moment — the common exception to -ment.
  11. die Firma — the exception to -ma.
  12. die Nacht — the only time of day that is not masculine.
  13. die Person — -ion is feminine; the others are young beings or -chen.

  15. Der Wein aus Italien ist sehr gut.
  16. Die Wohnung hat drei Zimmer.
  17. Das Auge tut weh.
  18. Wie ist der Name von deinem Lehrer?
  19. Das Hähnchen schmeckt lecker.
  20. Der See — a lake in the mountains.
  21. das Steuer — the steering wheel.
  22. Die Leiter — a ladder (and sie in the next sentence confirms it).
  23. Die Band — a music band.
