Monate und Wochentage

Months, weekdays and seasons — pronunciation, gender, abbreviations, the right preposition, beginning/middle/end, holidays and planning a week

Original topic sheet View "Monate des Jahres" in the Picture Book →

Month and day names look almost like English, which makes them easy to read — and easy to mispronounce or combine with the wrong preposition. Appointments, opening hours, timetables, birthdays and holidays all rely on these words, and they come up in every part of the A1 exam. This chapter covers the words themselves, how to say them, how to use them in sentences, and the calendar culture around them. Full dates with ordinal numbers (der 14. Mai) get their own chapter next (Chapter 44).

Watch the German sounds: J = English y, z = ts, s before a vowel = z, v in November = v (an exception — normally f). Juni and Juli sound so similar that on the phone Germans often say Juno and Julei to avoid confusion.

2. The seven days

die Wochentage — all masculine

der DonnerstagDoThursdaythunder (Thor's) day

der Samstag / SonnabendSaSaturdaySonnabend is used in northern Germany

In Germany the week starts on Monday, and calendars show it in the first column. Opening hours use the short forms: Mo–Fr 8–18 Uhr, Sa 9–14 Uhr, So geschlossen (closed).

3. Seasons and other time units

die Jahreszeiten

SeasonMonths (in Germany)Example
der Frühling (spring)März, April, MaiIm Frühling blühen die Blumen.
der Sommer (summer)Juni, Juli, AugustIm Sommer ist es warm.
der Herbst (autumn)September, Oktober, NovemberIm Herbst regnet es oft.
der Winter (winter)Dezember, Januar, FebruarIm Winter schneit es manchmal.

Seasons are masculine too — so months, days and seasons all take der. Other units: der Tag (day), die Woche (week), das Wochenende (weekend), der Monat (month), das Jahr (year), das Jahrhundert (century).

4. Prepositions: am or im?

Short units → am · long units → im

PrepositionWithExamples
am (= an dem)days, dates, the weekendam Montag, am 14. Mai, am Wochenende, am Feiertag
im (= in dem)months and seasonsim Mai, im Dezember, im Sommer, im Winter
in derweekin der Woche (on weekdays), in der nächsten Woche
no prepositionwith letzt- / nächst- / dies- / jed-nächsten Montag, letzten Juli, diesen Winter, jeden Sonntag
von ... bisrangesvon Montag bis Freitag, von Mai bis September
ab / seit / bisfrom / since / untilab Montag · seit Januar · bis Freitag

Why am and im and not just an/in? Because they're contractions with the dative article dem — and the article is masculine because all these nouns are masculine.

Once or every time?

am Montag = this/next Monday (one occasion) · montags = every Monday (small letter, see Chapter 38)
Am Samstag gehe ich auf eine Party. — Samstags gehe ich auf den Markt.
Combinations: am Montagmorgen (on Monday morning), sonntagabends (on Sunday evenings). Months don't have an -s form.

5. Beginning, middle, end

Anfang, Mitte, Ende — no preposition

  • Anfang Mai (at the beginning of May) — Ich fliege Anfang Mai nach Hause.
  • Mitte Juli (in mid-July) — Mitte Juli haben die Kinder Ferien.
  • Ende Dezember (at the end of December) — Der Kurs endet Ende Dezember.
  • Anfang der Woche / Ende der Woche (early / late in the week)
  • Anfang des Jahres / Ende des Monats — here with the Genitiv

Never „am Anfang Mai" or „im Ende Dezember" — before a month, Anfang/Mitte/Ende stand alone.

6. Asking about days and months

Questions and answers

Welcher Tag ist heute? (What day is it today?)Heute ist Mittwoch.

Note: Heute ist Montag without an article; but with a date: Heute ist der 14. Mai (Nominativ) and Wir haben den 14. Mai (Akkusativ, after haben). Details in Chapter 44.

7. Calendar culture

Working days, holidays, weekends

  • der Werktag (working day) — officially Monday to Saturday; der Arbeitstag usually Monday to Friday. „Lieferung in 3 Werktagen" (delivery in 3 working days).
  • der Feiertag (public holiday) — shops are closed. Nationwide: Neujahr (1.1.), Karfreitag and Ostermontag (Easter), Tag der Arbeit (1.5.), Christi Himmelfahrt, Pfingstmontag, Tag der Deutschen Einheit (3.10.), Weihnachten (25./26.12.). Some states have more.
  • der Sonntag is a quiet day: most shops are closed by law, and loud work (drilling, mowing the lawn) is not allowed — die Sonntagsruhe.
  • die Ferien (school holidays) are staggered by state, especially the Sommerferien.
  • der Brückentag ("bridge day") — a working day between a holiday and the weekend, popular for taking a day off.

Corrections to the topic sheet

  • „mit einem großem Buchstaben" → mit einem großen Buchstaben. After einem the adjective takes -en (Chapter 41).
  • The month list repeats the German name in brackets — „Januar (Januar)" — instead of the English. The table in section 1 gives the English and the pronunciation.
  • „Man benutzt: der + Datum" is only half the rule: the number is an ordinal with an ending (der vierzehnte Mai), and after am it becomes am vierzehnten Mai. See Chapter 44.
  • The sheet doesn't say the months and days are masculine — which is the reason for am and im.

Montags und mittwochs habe ich um 18 Uhr Deutschkurs. Am Dienstag muss ich um halb zehn zum Zahnarzt, und abends spiele ich Fußball. Donnerstag habe ich frei. Am Freitagabend gehe ich mit Jonas ins Kino. Samstags gehe ich immer auf den Markt, und diesen Samstag rufe ich meine Oma an, denn sie hat Geburtstag. Am Sonntag machen wir einen Ausflug an den See.

(On Mondays and Wednesdays I have German class at 6 p.m. On Tuesday I have to go to the dentist at half past nine, and in the evening I play football. I'm free on Thursday. On Friday evening I'm going to the cinema with Jonas. On Saturdays I always go to the market, and this Saturday I'm calling my grandma because it's her birthday. On Sunday we're going on a trip to the lake.)
