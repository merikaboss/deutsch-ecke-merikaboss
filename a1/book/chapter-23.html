Der Imperativ

Giving commands and instructions

Original topic sheet View "Imperative – Befehlssatz" in the Picture Book →

The imperative has three forms, matching the three "you"s from Chapter 6: informal singular (du), informal plural (ihr), and formal (Sie).

FormHow to build itgehen (to go)warten (to wait)
dustem only, no ending (drop -st)Geh!Warte!
ihrsame as the normal ihr-formGeht!Wartet!
Sieverb + Sie, same as normal Sie-formGehen Sie!Warten Sie!

Grammar rule: the verb always starts the sentence

Unlike statements (verb in position 2, Chapter 5), commands put the verb in position 1, with no subject at all for du and ihr — only the formal Sie form keeps its pronoun, right after the verb.

Vowel-changing verbs (Chapter 8) keep their change — except a → ä

Infinitivedu-form (normal)Imperative (du)
sprechen (e→i)du sprichstSprich!
lesen (e→ie)du liestLies!
fahren (a→ä, drops for imperative)du fährstFahr! (not "Fähr!")

Common mistake: keeping the "-st" ending

The du-imperative is built from the stem, not the full du-conjugation — drop the -st entirely: Geh!, not "Gehst!"

The irregular one: sein

duihrSie
Sei!Seid!Seien Sie!

Examples

  • Komm her! (Come here! — informal)
  • Bitte, warten Sie hier. (Please wait here. — formal)
  • Seid leise! (Be quiet! — to a group of friends/kids)
  • Hör bitte zu! (Please listen! — with separable verb zuhören, prefix still goes to the end)

Exercises

Try it yourself

1. Give the du-imperative of kommen (to come) and machen (to do).
2. Give the Sie-imperative of nehmen (to take).
3. Give the du-imperative of sprechen — remember the vowel change carries over.
4. Fix the mistake: "Gehst nach Hause!"
5. Translate politely, using Sie: "Please sit down." (sich setzen → Setzen Sie sich)
